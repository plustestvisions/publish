## Clear cut Conversation with LSD
### Communication
Two goals: information exchange and impression management aka learning and liking
So as a listener I have to understand the information and make the person sharing it feel as though you care.

#### Hearing hurdles
- You've got something to say
	- let people finish, then count to 3 if truly done, then summarize what you heard
- Your emotions take charge
	- let people finish without interruption then react
- You keep getting distracted
	- avoid distractions, if not possible, reschedule meeting
- You have the perfect advice
	- do not give the advice unless you are asked to, stick to words of comfort and questions that dig deeper
- You find the conversation dull
	- ask questions, get involved
- You think you already get it
	- Not: I know what you mean-> ask probing questions, take time to reflect on answers

##### Benefits of active listening
- stronger personal relationships
- more effective and meaningful interactions
- increased power of persuasion
- improved reputation
- wider access to information

Truly try to understand what someone is saying

### Ten-hut, listen up!
Hearing skills to be trained:
- Hearing - clean out that ear wax
- Auditory processing - decode what you heard
- Body language reading - mind tone of voice and social cues
- Maintaining attention - stay focused
- Regulating your emotional response - keep calm
- Integrating multiple resources of information - get organising
- Body language communication - maintain eye contact and speak with your body- 

Reason for listening: think briefly on what goal you have with a conversation and how to listen to it. Is the speaker aiming for feedback or an emotional connection?
Believe that what they say is important, you do not need to agree.
So aim for  the what AND the why

Secret weapons
W.A.I.T. - Why Am I Talking
The Improv Approach - pretend you are doing improv and can only react in the moment.
	Improv is about listening and tailoring your response to what the other person said
### Move your body
two purposes of listening: learning and liking
body language: voice inflection (stembuiging), tone, facial expressions, gestures, posture (houding) will help you especially in the liking part.

- Eye contact: no laptop, no phone/tablet. Calmly look the person in the eyes
- Calm: frequent or fast movements indicate losing focus
- Facial expressions: try to mirror the expressions of the speakers face
- Posture: active posture leaning slightly towards the other person, no crossing arms of fidgeting
- Affirmation: nodding, yes, affirmative sounds, but do not over do it

Notice the speaker:
- Notice what the speaker is saying, Notice the words
- Notice the way the speaker is saying it, voice
- Notice the manner in which they are saying it, what does their posture and body language tell you
- Where is the speaker looking? eye contact yes or no?
- Ask yourself what the speaker is feeling now, and try to analyze their point of view

> [!tip] 
> Make notes 

- Prepare before the meeting what notes you gonna make.
- Focus on main argument and use short sentences
- Read them directly after conversation

## Summarise
**Goals**
- Play It Back for 'Em: learn how to prove your actively listening
- The power of silence: A well executed pause can say more then words ever could
- Compassionate Conversations: Making someone feel heard is great, but there's more you can do
### Play it back
- Structures
	- summarizing provides a way to structure the received information
- Stimulates
	- The thinking process of the speaker can advance by summarizing by the public
- Stops
	- You can stop the speaker from speaking, which gives the speaker time to recapture, have a rest and let him/her feel heard and to encourage to continue the talk
- Steers
	- Earlier you mentioned that seems important, let's zoom in too that.

A good summary will build self esteem of the speaker.

#### So if I understood correctly
positive atmosphere, pick right moments to summarize, and leave room to have the speaker correct you, do it in your own words (no conclusions, not the obvisious)
Paraphrasing/repeating what you have heard in your own words, when speaker has fallen silent, something is unclear or to inform the speaker that the message is well received.

### The power of silence
pauses during conversations are equally important as what you say
in emotional situations frequent and longer pauses gives the other person(s) time to reflect on them
pay attention to your own body language during a pause in the conversation, especially eyes and facial expression.

### Compassionate conversations
Empathy unveiled
Take your time and  Focus on the conversation
Treat everyone differently
Be patient
- Avoid interruptions
- Take slow deep breaths, stay calm
- pause before responding
- keep body language open and use welcoming gestures
- allow comfortable silences to let the other person collect their thoughts
- match the speakers pace.
Do not judge
- avoid making assumptions about intentions-> questions to ask
- focus on understanding the speakers viewpoint
- maintain neutral body language
- ask for context and background when something is unclear or surprising
- acknowledge and validate emotions without imposing judgements on them
- focus on concrete facts and examples to avoid relying solely on personal opinions
- stay curious and approach the conversation as an opportunity to learn and explore

## Dig Deeper

### Purpose of questioning
> [!important] 
> Every question is a cry to understand the world, so there are no dumb questions 

Why are people fail to not ask questions?
- too focused on themselves
- not caring enough to ask
- overconfident in their own knowledge
- think you get bored by the answers
- they do not know about good questioning
> [!tip] 
> - [ ] remember your listener role->it is about them, not you
> - [ ] know that boredome in a conversation can be cured by participation
> - [ ] if you want to give advice or solution, phrase it as question: I wonder what would happen if you fix X?

Read the signs?
> [!tip] 
> - find clues to further topics & questions- not cues for a matching story
> - beforehand consider what you want to know and which questions to ask in order to find that out
> - take careful note when your partner expresses a subjective opinion, remains vague, or makes assumptions, these often call for clarification, so ask more questions
> - pick up on what your partner is saying, but also notice what is left unsaid.
> - observe body language

### How can I put this?
Tone of voice!!!Casual opens up people willingness to answer
When you ask questions

> [!tip] 
> - summarize what you heard before asking a clarifying question
> - do not rehearse your question while they are still talking
> - avoid interrupting, ask during natural pauses
> - wait a few seconds before asking the question that came automatically
> - offer an out with difficult questions: Perhaps I am misundertanding something, Maybe I misheard, I could be missing something....

soften commands: What you said earlier about XYZ really stuck me, can we go back to that real quick?
#### Group dynamics
Make people feel comfortable by asking questions in a group that you know includes people that will respond.
Pace your conversations and stay on track on your goal, bear in mind group dynamics.

### What can I say?

Open questions
keeps the conversation flowing, inviting any kind of answer beyond yes/no
	What was the best part of your day? What should we prioritize in the budget?
Closed questions
you ask when you need specific information
	Did you have a good day? Is there room in the budget for X?
Types of questions
- Follow up
	- shows your paying attention, interested in learning more
	- to obtain extra information
	- they mostly come automatically
- Summative
	- to check if your conversation partner is clear on everything and get their opinion on it
	- does this make sense to you?
	- so do we agree that this is the best solution?
- Reflective
	- to check whether you understood the speaker correctly 
	- summarize the others emotion or impression
	- So this working well as far as you're concerned?
	- I get the impression you are angry, is that the case?
- Suggestive
	- Imply the expected answer
	- be careful could be inappropriate 
	- to seduce someone or confirm a shared goal
	- Don't you want us to become a market leader in our industry?
	- Aren't you the obvious person for this role?
- Probing
	- aims to clarify something that was said
	- Can you give me an example of that
	- Could you please expand a bit on that point?
	- Can you explain what you meant by that?
	- understanding what they are trying to say
- Restricted response
	- offer a choice of answers
	- Are you for or against?
	- Are you married, not married or divorced
- Challenging
	- if the speaker shared a statement or opinion that deserves more reflection
		- imagine a conversation with a collegae who's been very touchy lately, wo you confront him with it when he says he does not get well with a direct collegae: is your collegae the only problem?
		- So if you find a different co worker, you'd be happy again?
- Hypothetical
	- pose an assumption as a way of stimulating the others thinking
		- what would you do if you won 1 million euros
		- if you were the ceo of this company, what would you change?

#### Organize a speed date
kinda of liberating structure in pairs about a passion and the other one ask questions to uncover:- 
- when it started?
- what sparked it?
- how it evolved?
- what challenges were faced
- any significant achievements or events associated with it
	- But first team member tells his/her passion in one sentence
	- Then for two minutes the other one asks questions to get above information
	- Reverse and repeat until everyone has paired up
- How did everyone experienced the speed dates?
- which questions helped them open up?
- What questions yielded most insightful responses?
- what new things did they learn about each other?

#### Create a question ladder
1. set a timer for 2 minutes
2. grab a piece of paper on top write down the topic you are interested in
3. write down first generic question that popups up in your head reagrding the topic
4. based on first question, which next deeper question comes up
5. now depending on the second question which third even deeper question do you want to know the answer to
6. continu until the timer finishes, try to make questions more detailed
- What did you do to make the questions deeper?
- Now do an online search to get your answers
- What questions gave you the most accurate answers you were looking for?
- What new things did you learn
- What information were you still missing?


## Listening in the workspace

Results in creativity, innovation and productivity amongst teams.

Dos & Don'ts
- Respect your colleagues
- Ask opinions
- practise mindfulness during conversations
- turn away from tech during calls

Do not assume you have all the answers, and do not shut out people from planning and do not multi taks, focus on the conversation

Listening to de-escalate and by asking questions
Tough talks start with the tough topic first
Deep listening: 1 purpose->to help your partner to empty their heart
