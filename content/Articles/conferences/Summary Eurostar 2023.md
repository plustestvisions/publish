## The main take aways from Eurostar:
- It is all about the people
- Invest in your team to get collaborating more and better
- Watch the language you use
- Active listening is very important to know what the problem of your colleague(s) is/are then search for common problems and work on it together.
- Know your environment around your team and know who can help you
- Critical thinking is essential

## Critical Thinking
Definitions:
**Inference** is something we treat as true based on evidence
**assumption** is something that we treat as true even though we have insufficient evidence
**premise** is an assumption that begins a chain of reasoning. All logic is based on premises

Testers question assumptions & premises and gather data for better inferences

#### Architecture
Thinking on architecture is greatly simulated using this nice game. Came to know I have should played this game more with my nephew ;-)
At first I was convinced your first design should be simple, but actually there was one team that had a very nice compact and simple design in the first round, but after round 2 they transformed it into a really impressive complex system too. In the first round I noticed that all teams (except one) where designing a similar system/solution. The next round introduced new techniques a booster, a second level and in the last session some more complexity is added. Like in real life it becomes more difficult to integrate new stuff into your initial architecture. So in the starting phase I think you should consider as much different scenario's you know and also need to be aware of weaknesses and strong points too. Be realistic you can not foresee everything.
